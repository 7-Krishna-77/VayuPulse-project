Technical Education & FAQ

Understanding the chemistry and medical pathways behind vehicle emissions and public health.

PM2.5 refers to atmospheric particulate matter that has a diameter of less than 2.5 micrometers (about 3% the diameter of a human hair). Because of their tiny size, these particles remain suspended in the air longer and can penetrate deep into human lung tissue and cross into the bloodstream, linked to premature cardiovascular and respiratory ailments.
Nitrogen Oxides (NO and NO2) are produced during high-temperature combustion in engine cylinders. When exposed to solar ultraviolet radiation and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in stagnant urban air, they trigger photo-oxidation reactions forming ground-level Ozone (O3) and haze, aggravating respiratory tracts and reducing crop yields.
Inhaled fine particles trigger systemic inflammation and oxidative stress in blood vessels. Over prolonged exposures, this accelerates arterial plaque buildup (atherosclerosis), elevates blood pressure, and increases risk rates for myocardial infarctions and stroke among urban dwellers residing near arterial highways.
